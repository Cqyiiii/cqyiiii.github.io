In brief

Train a solver to finish correct solutions under a hard token cap, then freeze it. Score each answer by its most surprising tokens and calibrate a cutoff on labeled problems to control wrong submissions.

Training dynamics

Why decouple solving from abstention?

With a penalty for a wrong answer, an unfinished attempt can outscore a wrong submission. The theory isolates when attempts dry up before the solver improves. Training logs show how that incentive appears in practice.

Practical takeaways
API and white-box scores
Returned token log probabilities suffice for the default score. A white-box entropy variant needs full-vocabulary probabilities and an additional offline pass.
Cost of target recalibration
At the study’s single-seed 8B 4K generation rate, scoring 300 labeled target problems costs about USD 0.73. This estimate covers generation and assumes reference labels are available.
When Native 8K competes
Uninterrupted 8B decoding already solves 35.4% of DAPO with 97.8% precision. The value of a tighter cap depends on the cost of withholding and of mistakes.
Scope of the guarantee
The bound controls expected wrong submissions per problem for a fixed solver and score under exchangeability. A particular test set can exceed its nominal target.
